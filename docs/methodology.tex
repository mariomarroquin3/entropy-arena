\begin{abstract}
\noindent
\emph{Entropy Arena} is an experimental framework that compares randomness
sources --- computational, mathematical, physical and manual --- as suppliers of
seeds and private keys for a $2$-of-$3$ multisignature Bitcoin wallet. Its
central thesis is that \emph{statistical resemblance to randomness and
unpredictability are different properties}: a deterministic process, however
chaotic, inherits all of its unpredictability from the seed that starts it. We
formalize this with two independent evaluation axes: a \emph{statistical
quality} $Q$, obtained from the NIST SP~800-22 battery aggregated by the
proportion-of-passes and $p$-value-uniformity criteria, and a \emph{real
entropy} $r$, the number of unpredictable bits actually injected per sample,
estimated with the min-entropy estimators of NIST SP~800-90B. We derive every
metric, explain why each is used and what it cannot detect, describe the
physics and dynamics of each generator and why it does or does not supply
entropy, present concrete attacks that break generators that pass the whole
battery (Mersenne Twister, RANDU, digits of $\pi$, clock seeds), and give the
cryptographic pipeline (BIP39/32/48/67, descriptors) that turns the measured
entropy into a verified regtest multisignature transaction. We also correct a
common misconception on threshold security: in a $t$-of-$n$ scheme the security
is set by the $t$-th weakest key, not by the strongest one.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Threat Model}
% ============================================================

The security of a cryptographic key is the probability that an adversary
guesses it. That probability depends on \emph{what the adversary knows}, not on
how the key ``looks''. We fix the following threat model, used throughout.

\begin{definition}[Adversary]\label{def:adv}
The adversary $\mathcal{A}$ knows the source code and hardware of every
generator, can observe any previous output of the generator (but not the
private inputs of the current sample), and can test $q$ candidates offline.
\end{definition}

Let $\mathcal{M}=\{M_1,\dots,M_k\}$ be a set of entropy methods. A method
consumes a possibly empty \emph{fresh unpredictable input} $\omega$ (an OS
seed, a physical measurement, dice rolls) and produces a byte string
\begin{equation}
    D = M(\omega, n) \in \{0,1\}^{8n}.
\end{equation}
The arena assigns to each method two \emph{independent} quantities:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
    \item the \textbf{statistical quality} $Q(M)\in[0,100]$: how well $D$
          resembles a uniform i.i.d.\ string under a battery of tests;
    \item the \textbf{real entropy} $r(M)\ge 0$ (bits): how much unpredictability
          $\omega$ injects, conditionally on Definition~\ref{def:adv}.
\end{itemize}
The reason for two axes is a theorem (Section~\ref{sec:conservation}): $Q$ can
be made maximal by any deterministic function, while $r$ cannot exceed the
entropy of $\omega$. A single weighted score would blur this distinction; the
first version of this project used one, and we replaced it (Section~\ref{sec:quality}).

% ============================================================
\section{Preliminaries: Measures of Entropy}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Shannon, collision and min-entropy}

Let $X$ be a discrete random variable with pmf $p(x)$ on a finite alphabet
$\mathcal{X}$.

\begin{definition}[R\'enyi entropies]
\begin{align}
    H_1(X) &= -\sum_x p(x)\log_2 p(x) &&\text{(Shannon)},\label{eq:shannon}\\
    H_2(X) &= -\log_2 \sum_x p(x)^2 &&\text{(collision)},\label{eq:collision}\\
    \Hmin(X) &= -\log_2 \max_x p(x) &&\text{(min-entropy)}.\label{eq:minentropy}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Ordering]\label{prop:order}
$\Hmin(X)\;\le\;H_2(X)\;\le\;H_1(X)\;\le\;\log_2|\mathcal{X}|$, and
$H_2(X)\le 2\,\Hmin(X)$. All are equal iff $X$ is uniform on its support.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $\sum_x p(x)^2 \le \max_x p(x)\sum_x p(x)=\max_x p(x)$, we get
$H_2\ge\Hmin$; since $\sum_x p(x)^2\ge(\max_x p(x))^2$, we get $H_2\le 2\Hmin$.
Jensen's inequality for the concave $\log$ gives $H_1\ge H_2$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why min-entropy is the cryptographic measure.} If the adversary makes
$q$ guesses, each guess succeeds with probability at most $\max_x p(x)$, so
\begin{equation}
    \Pr[\mathcal{A}\text{ succeeds}] \;\le\; q\,2^{-\Hmin(X)}.
\end{equation}
Shannon entropy averages over outcomes and can be large while one outcome is
very likely; the adversary will simply try that outcome first.
\begin{example}
Let $X=0$ with probability $1/2$ and uniform on $2^{100}$ other values
otherwise. Then $H_1\approx 51$ bits, but $\Hmin=1$: half of the keys are
guessed in one attempt.
\end{example}

\subsection{Conditional min-entropy and conservation}\label{sec:conservation}

\begin{definition}
For a random variable $D$ and adversary knowledge $Z$,
\begin{equation}
\Hmin(D\mid Z)=-\log_2\,\mathbb{E}_z\!\left[\max_d \Pr[D=d\mid Z=z]\right].
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Entropy conservation]\label{thm:conservation}
Let $D=g(S)$ where $g$ is a deterministic function known to the adversary and
$S$ is the random seed. Then $\Hmin(D\mid g)\le\Hmin(S)$. Consequently a
deterministic generator with an $s$-bit seed can never contribute more than
$s$ bits, regardless of its statistical properties.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $s^\ast$ be a most likely seed. Then
$\Pr[D=g(s^\ast)]\ge\Pr[S=s^\ast]=\max_s\Pr[S=s]$, hence
$\max_d\Pr[D=d]\ge 2^{-\Hmin(S)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:conservation} is the data-processing inequality for
min-entropy. It explains why applying SHA-256 to a low-entropy input gives an
output that is statistically perfect (Shannon entropy $\approx 8$ bits/byte,
all tests passed) but has $\Hmin\le\Hmin(\text{input})$: hashing \emph{hides}
structure, it does not \emph{create} uncertainty.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Real entropy $r$]
For a method, $r(M)$ is the average, over samples, of $\min\{b(M),8n\}$, where
$b(M)$ is the number of bits of fresh unpredictable input consumed to produce
the sample, conditional on Definition~\ref{def:adv} (that is, given the
algorithm and all previous outputs).
\end{definition}

Examples of $b$: a CSPRNG gives $8n$; a mathematical map seeded with 8 OS bytes
gives $64$; the Mersenne Twister that persists between samples gives $0$,
because its state is recoverable from earlier outputs (Section~\ref{sec:attacks});
a manual source gives the measured min-entropy of the recorded rolls.

% ============================================================
\section{Entropy Sources: Mathematics and Physics}
% ============================================================

Methods fall into four families: \emph{computational}, \emph{mathematical},
\emph{physical} and \emph{manual/hybrid}.

\subsection{Computational sources}

\paragraph{CSPRNG (\texttt{secrets}, \texttt{os.urandom}).}
The operating system collects physical noise (interrupt timing, hardware RNG
instructions, device jitter) into an entropy pool and expands it with a
cryptographic generator $G:\{0,1\}^\lambda\to\{0,1\}^n$. If $G$ is a secure PRG,
\begin{equation}
    \big|\Pr[\mathcal{A}(G(U_\lambda))=1]-\Pr[\mathcal{A}(U_n)=1]\big|\le\varepsilon(\lambda)
\end{equation}
for every polynomial-time $\mathcal{A}$. Its power comes from a \emph{fresh
secret seed} of $\lambda\ge 128$ bits, not from the shape of the output.

\paragraph{Mersenne Twister (MT19937).}
The state is $\mathbf{x}\in(\mathbb{F}_2)^{19937}$ (624 words of 32 bits, less
31 unused bits), and one step is a \emph{linear} map over $\mathbb{F}_2$:
\begin{equation}
    x_{k+624}=x_{k+397}\oplus\big((x_k^{u}\,\|\,x_{k+1}^{l})A\big),
\end{equation}
followed by the tempering $y\mapsto y\oplus(y\!\gg\!11)$,
$y\oplus((y\!\ll\!7)\wedge\mathrm{0x9D2C5680})$,
$y\oplus((y\!\ll\!15)\wedge\mathrm{0xEFC60000})$, $y\oplus(y\!\gg\!18)$, also
linear over $\mathbb{F}_2$ and invertible. The period $2^{19937}-1$ and 623-dim
equidistribution~\cite{mt98} explain why it passes statistical batteries; the
linearity explains why it is predictable (Section~\ref{sec:attacks}).

\paragraph{RANDU.} The linear congruential generator
$x_{k+1}=65539\,x_k \bmod 2^{31}$. Since $65539=2^{16}+3$,
$65539^2\equiv 6\cdot65539-9 \pmod{2^{31}}$, hence
\begin{equation}
    x_{k+2}=6x_{k+1}-9x_k \pmod{2^{31}}.
\end{equation}
Any three consecutive outputs satisfy $9x_k-6x_{k+1}+x_{k+2}\equiv0$, so the
points $(x_k,x_{k+1},x_{k+2})$ lie on at most $15$ planes~\cite{marsaglia68}. The
generator keeps a 31-bit state (30 bits for an odd seed), so at most $30$ bits
of entropy exist whatever the output looks like.

\paragraph{PCG64 (\texttt{numpy.random.default\_rng}).} A statistical (not
cryptographic) generator seeded from a 128-bit \texttt{SeedSequence} drawn from
the OS. Fresh seeding per sample gives $b=128$; the generator itself is
invertible, so seed reuse across samples would give $0$.

\paragraph{UUIDv4.} $122$ of the $128$ bits are random; $6$ encode version and
variant. Hence $b=n\cdot 8\cdot 122/128$. The constant bits are visible to
statistical tests: they bias the monobit, runs, cumulative-sums and chi-squared
statistics, so UUIDv4 has good entropy but poor $Q$ (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\subsection{Mathematical sources: deterministic dynamics}

Every source in this family is a map $s_{k+1}=f(s_k)$ with a seed $s_0$ drawn
from the OS (64 bits per sample); by Theorem~\ref{thm:conservation}
$b\le 64$ regardless of dynamics. What differs is \emph{how much of the seed's
information the output reveals}, which we analyze per system.

\subsubsection{Brownian motion}

Physically, a Brownian particle is driven by molecular collisions:
the Langevin equation $m\dot v=-\gamma v+\sqrt{2\gamma k_BT}\,\eta(t)$ has a
truly stochastic term $\eta$ whose source is thermal (a quantum-statistical
ensemble at temperature $T$). The Wiener process $W_t$ has independent Gaussian
increments $W_t-W_s\sim\mathcal{N}(0,t-s)$. \emph{In the arena, however, the
noise is simulated}:
\begin{equation}
    \xi_k=\sqrt{\Delta t}\,Z_k,\quad Z_k\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)\ \text{drawn from a PRNG},\qquad
    b_k=\mathbf{1}[\xi_k>0].
\end{equation}
Each $b_k$ is a deterministic function of the PRNG seed, so the physical
randomness of the \emph{model} is irrelevant: the simulation has only the seed's
entropy. This is a recurring lesson: \emph{simulating a random physical process
is not measuring one.}

\subsubsection{Logistic map}

$x_{k+1}=r x_k(1-x_k)$. For $r=4$ the map is conjugate to the doubling map:
with $x=\sin^2(\pi\theta)$, $\theta_{k+1}=2\theta_k \bmod 1$. Writing
$\theta_0=0.d_1d_2d_3\ldots$ in binary,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{1}[x_k>\tfrac12]=\mathbf{1}[\theta_k\in(\tfrac14,\tfrac34)]=d_{k+1}\oplus d_{k+2}.
\end{equation}
The output is a linear ($\mathbb{F}_2$) filter of the binary digits of the seed:
the chaotic map \emph{spends} one seed bit per iteration (Lyapunov exponent
$\lambda=\ln 2$, i.e.\ Kolmogorov--Sinai entropy of $1$ bit/iteration), it does
not create any. In double precision the state has only $53$ bits, so the computed orbit is a
deterministic function of at most $53$ bits of the seed and is eventually
periodic; after $\approx53$ iterations the extracted bits reflect
floating-point rounding, not new information.

\paragraph{Threshold bias.} At $r=3.99$ the invariant density is no longer
symmetric about $1/2$, so the threshold bit $\mathbf{1}[x>1/2]$ is biased.
This was the reason the first implementation scored poorly ($Q\approx24$); the
current implementation extracts a low-order bit,
$b_k=\lfloor 2^{30}x_k\rfloor \bmod 2$, which is insensitive to the coarse
density.

\subsubsection{Lorenz attractor}

The flow $\dot x=\sigma(y-x)$, $\dot y=x(\rho-z)-y$, $\dot z=xy-\beta z$
($\sigma=10,\rho=28,\beta=8/3$) has largest Lyapunov exponent
$\lambda_1\approx0.9056$~\cite{lorenz63}. The Kolmogorov--Sinai entropy rate is
the sum of positive exponents, $h_{KS}\approx\lambda_1/\ln2\approx1.31$
bits per unit of time. With explicit Euler steps $\Delta t=10^{-2}$ we extract
one bit per step,
\begin{equation}
    b_k=\mathbf{1}[x_{k+1}>x_k],
\end{equation}
but the system generates only $h_{KS}\Delta t\approx0.013$ bits per step:
the source is \emph{oversampled by a factor of $\approx75$}. Consecutive bits
are the sign of a slowly varying derivative and are strongly correlated (runs
of equal bits), which is why Lorenz fails four tests and has min-entropy
$\approx1.2$ bits/byte. The physical lesson: a chaotic system produces
information at a finite rate; sampling faster than that rate reads the same
information repeatedly.

\subsubsection{Rule 30 cellular automaton}

$c_i^{(t+1)}=c_{i-1}^{(t)}\oplus(c_i^{(t)}\lor c_{i+1}^{(t)})$. Wolfram proposed
the central column as a generator~\cite{wolfram86}: bits are extracted from
$c_{\lfloor L/2\rfloor}^{(t)}$, $t>K$. The rule is nonlinear (the $\lor$),
which is why it resists linear-complexity attacks, and the column passes the
tests, but the initial ring (of $L=256$ cells) is filled from a 64-bit seed, so
$b=64$. Our first version extracted only the first byte of each row, a poor
choice that failed tests ($Q\approx40$); using the central column fixes the
quality without adding entropy.

\subsubsection{Binary digits of $\pi$}

$\pi$ is conjectured normal (every binary block occurs with the expected
frequency) and passes the tests, but any prefix is computable by a fixed
program (we use the Chudnovsky series): its Kolmogorov complexity is
$O(\log n)$. The only entropy is the choice of the starting offset:
$b=\log_2(N_\pi-8n)\approx 22.9$ bits for $N_\pi=8\cdot10^6$ bits.

\subsection{Physical and manual sources}

\subsubsection{CPU timing jitter}

The delta of a high-resolution clock around a fixed workload varies because of
physical effects: oscillator phase noise (thermal and shot noise in the PLL),
cache and branch-predictor state, interrupts, bus arbitration. The least
significant bit of the delta (after dividing by the clock granularity, which on
Windows is $100$ ns) carries a small amount of entropy per sample but is
\emph{biased and serially correlated}: the raw stream fails the NIST battery
($Q\approx20$). Conditioning with a hash (SHA-256 over blocks) gives
$Q\approx100$, but the entropy claimed must remain the \emph{measured}
min-entropy of the raw stream (Section~\ref{sec:90b}), otherwise the hash would
manufacture apparent entropy.

\subsubsection{Dice and coins}

Classical dice and coins are deterministic mechanical systems; unpredictability
comes from \emph{sensitivity to initial conditions} amplified by the chaotic
bounces, so that a human throw cannot be reproduced. Two caveats:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
    \item \textbf{Coins} are not fair in general: Diaconis, Holmes and
    Montgomery~\cite{dhm07} show that a flipped coin lands on the starting side
    with probability $\approx0.508$ (dynamical bias from precession). A bias
    $p$ gives $\Hmin=-\log_2\max(p,1-p)<1$ bit per flip.
    \item \textbf{Dice} have manufacturing asymmetries (drilled pips, rounded
    edges) and human habits (avoiding repeats), lowering $\Hmin$ below
    $\log_2 6\approx2.585$ bits.
\end{itemize}
For $n$ recorded outcomes of a symbol source we do not assume uniformity; we
estimate the min-entropy with the upper confidence bound
\begin{equation}
    \hat p_u=\min\!\Big\{1,\;\hat p_{\max}+2.576\sqrt{\tfrac{\hat p_{\max}(1-\hat p_{\max})}{n-1}}\Big\},
    \qquad b=n\cdot\min\{\log_2 |\mathcal A|,\,-\log_2\hat p_u\}.
    \label{eq:manual_bits}
\end{equation}
For example, $100$ rolls of a fair die give $\hat p_u\approx0.26$, i.e.\
$\approx1.9$ bits/roll rather than $2.585$: a small sample cannot certify the
nominal entropy, and the honest estimate is lower. To reach $128$ bits at the
nominal rate one needs $\lceil128/\log_2 6\rceil=50$ rolls; in practice more,
and the diagnostic script reports whether the measured value passes $128$.
The recorded outcomes are compressed to bytes with SHA-256 iterated, which is
sound only because $b$ is taken from the measurement, not from the output.

\subsubsection{Deck shuffle}

A uniform permutation of $52$ cards has $\log_2(52!)\approx225.58$ bits, encoded
by the Lehmer code $N=\sum_i c_i(52-i)!$ with $c_i=|\{j>i:\pi_j<\pi_i\}|$
(a bijection onto $\{0,\dots,52!-1\}$). Humans do not shuffle uniformly:
Bayer and Diaconis~\cite{bd92} show that about seven riffle shuffles are needed
for near-uniformity in total variation. The $225.58$ bits are an upper bound,
not a measurement.

\subsubsection{von Neumann extractor and XOR combination}

For a coin with $\Pr[H]=p\neq1/2$ and independent flips, taking disjoint pairs
$(H,T)\mapsto1$, $(T,H)\mapsto0$, discarding $(H,H),(T,T)$ yields exactly fair
bits~\cite{vn51}: both accepted pairs have probability $p(1-p)$. The
acceptance rate per pair is $2p(1-p)$, so $\mathbb{E}[\text{flips per bit}]=1/(p(1-p))\ge4$.
Independence between flips is essential; correlated flips break the guarantee.

The XOR of independent sources satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \Hmin(X_1\oplus\cdots\oplus X_m)\;\ge\;\max_j\Hmin(X_j),
\end{equation}
so combining \texttt{secrets} with dice cannot hurt: if the dice are poor, the
CSPRNG still gives $256$ bits; if the CSPRNG were compromised, the dice still
contribute. The bound is a maximum, not a sum, and requires independence.

% ============================================================
\section{Evaluation Metrics}\label{sec:metrics}
% ============================================================

We use three families of metrics. Each answers a different question and has a
blind spot that the others cover.

\subsection{NIST SP~800-22 statistical tests}\label{sec:nist}
The tests and aggregation criteria follow NIST SP~800-22~\cite{sp22}.

\paragraph{Framework.} A test evaluates the null hypothesis $H_0$: ``the bits
are i.i.d.\ Bernoulli($1/2$)'' via a statistic with known distribution and
returns a $p$-value. Under $H_0$ the $p$-value is uniform on $[0,1]$, so the
test rejects an ideal source with probability $\alpha=0.01$. \emph{Passing a
test is a failure to reject $H_0$, not a proof of randomness}; each test only
probes one class of alternatives.

Let the bit expansion be $\mathbf{b}\in\{0,1\}^m$, $m=8n$, and
$X_i=2b_i-1$.

\paragraph{Monobit.} $s_{\rm obs}=|\sum_iX_i|/\sqrt m$, $p=\erfc(s_{\rm obs}/\sqrt2)$.
Detects a global bias of ones and zeros.

\paragraph{Runs.} With $\pi=\frac1m\sum_ib_i$ and
$V=1+\sum_{i}\mathbf{1}[b_i\ne b_{i+1}]$,
\begin{equation}
    p=\erfc\!\left(\frac{|V-2m\pi(1-\pi)|}{2\sqrt{2m}\,\pi(1-\pi)}\right),
\end{equation}
provided $|\pi-\frac12|<2/\sqrt m$. Detects too-fast or too-slow oscillation
(serial dependence at short range).

\paragraph{Block frequency.} For blocks of $L$ bits with ones-proportion $\pi_i$,
$\chi^2=4L\sum_{i=1}^{M}(\pi_i-\frac12)^2\sim\chi^2_M$, $p=\Pr[\chi^2_M\ge\chi^2]$
(here $L=128$). Detects local biases that cancel globally.

\paragraph{Cumulative sums (forward and backward).} With $S_k=\sum_{i\le k}X_i$
and $z=\max_k|S_k|$, the $p$-value follows the distribution of the maximum of
a random walk (NIST~\S2.13), evaluated with the normal CDF $\Phi$ over the
finite ranges of $k$. Detects drift of the walk.

\paragraph{Longest run of ones in a block.} Blocks of $M=8$ bits are classified
by longest run ($\le1$, $2$, $3$, $\ge4$) with null proportions
$(0.2148,0.3672,0.2305,0.1875)$, and
$\chi^2=\sum_j(v_j-N\pi_j)^2/(N\pi_j)\sim\chi^2_3$.

\paragraph{Chi-squared on bytes.} With $c_v$ the count of byte value $v$ and
$E_v=n/256$,
$\chi^2=\sum_{v=0}^{255}(c_v-E_v)^2/E_v\sim\chi^2_{255}$. The chi-squared
approximation requires $E_v\ge5$, i.e.\ $n\ge1280$; with our $n=12\,500$,
$E_v\approx48.8$. (The original design used $n=256$, giving $E_v=1$, an invalid
test; this was corrected.)

\subsubsection{Aggregation across samples}

One sample produces one $p$-value per test; a single $p$-value from one string
cannot separate a good generator from a lucky bad one. NIST~\S4.2 therefore
evaluates $N$ independent samples with two criteria per test.

\begin{definition}[Proportion criterion]
The number of passing samples is Binomial$(N,1-\alpha)$ under $H_0$; the source
passes if the observed proportion $\hat\pi\ge\hat p_0-3\sqrt{\hat p_0(1-\hat p_0)/N}$,
with $\hat p_0=1-\alpha$. For $N=100$ the bound is $0.960$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Uniformity criterion]
Under $H_0$ the $p$-values are uniform. Bin the $N$ $p$-values in $10$ equal
intervals with counts $F_i$ and compute
$\chi^2=\sum_{i=1}^{10}(F_i-N/10)^2/(N/10)$, $P_T=\Pr[\chi^2_9\ge\chi^2]$.
The source passes if $P_T\ge10^{-4}$ (requires $N\ge55$).
\end{definition}

A test is \emph{OK} only if \emph{both} criteria hold. The second criterion
catches sources that pass at $\hat\pi=0.99$ but whose $p$-values crowd near
$0.01$--$0.05$ (a mild but systematic defect). We use $N=100$ samples of
$8n=10^5$ bits (n = 12\,500 bytes).

\paragraph{Limits.} With seven tests and two criteria each, an ideal source
fails at least one criterion with non-negligible probability. In our runs a
\texttt{secrets}-like source sometimes has one test out of seven rejected
(a $Q\approx90$ result); this is statistical noise, not a defect, which is why
the verdict tolerates a single rejection.

\subsection{Min-entropy estimation (NIST SP~800-90B)}\label{sec:90b}

The tests above examine the distribution of outputs; they cannot bound the
adversary's guessing probability. SP~800-90B~\cite{sp90b} estimates min-entropy
per symbol of a non-i.i.d.\ source by running \emph{ten estimators} and
taking the \emph{minimum}: each estimator is sound against one kind of
structure and blind to the others. On a bit sequence $x_1,\dots,x_L$ the
estimators we implement are:

\paragraph{1. Most Common Value (MCV).} With $\hat p$ the frequency of the most
common symbol,
\begin{equation}
p_u=\min\Big\{1,\hat p+2.576\sqrt{\tfrac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}{L-1}}\Big\},\qquad H=-\log_2p_u.
\end{equation}
It sees only the marginal frequency.

\paragraph{2. Collision.} Scan for consecutive collisions. In the binary case
the time to the next repeated symbol is $t\in\{2,3\}$ with
$\mathbb{E}[t]=2+2pq$ ($q=1-p$). With the sample mean $\bar t$, sample standard
deviation $\hat\sigma$ over $v$ collisions, the lower confidence bound
$\bar t'=\bar t-2.576\hat\sigma/\sqrt v$ gives
$pq=(\bar t'-2)/2$, $p=\tfrac12\big(1+\sqrt{1-4pq}\big)$, $H=-\log_2p$.

\paragraph{3. Markov.} Estimate the initial distribution and the $2\times2$
transition matrix; let $p_{\max}$ be the probability of the most likely
$128$-bit chain (dynamic programming). Then
$H=\min\{1,\,-\log_2p_{\max}/128\}$.

\paragraph{4. Compression (Maurer).} Partition into $b=6$-bit blocks; with
$D_i$ the distance to the previous occurrence of block $i$ (a dictionary of
$d=1000$ initial blocks discarded), $\bar X=\overline{\log_2D_i}$,
$\hat\sigma=0.5907\,\mathrm{sd}(\log_2D_i)$, $\bar X'=\bar X-2.576\hat\sigma/\sqrt{\nu}$.
Solve $\mathbb{E}(p)=\bar X'$ for $p$, where
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}(p)=G(p)+(2^b-1)G(q),\ q=\tfrac{1-p}{2^b-1},\quad
    G(z)=\tfrac1\nu\!\!\sum_{t=d+1}^{L}\!\Big[\sum_{u=1}^{t-1}\!\log_2u\,z^2(1-z)^{u-1}
    +\log_2t\,z(1-z)^{t-1}\Big],
\end{equation}
and $H=-\log_2p/b$. It bounds how compressible the source is~\cite{maurer92}, the information-theoretic view of redundancy.

\paragraph{5. $t$-Tuple.} Let $Q_i$ be the count of the most common $i$-tuple and
$t$ the largest $i$ with $Q_i\ge35$. With $P_i=Q_i/(L-i+1)$,
$p=\max_{i\le t}P_i^{1/i}$ and $H=-\log_2\min\{1,p+2.576\sqrt{p(1-p)/(L-1)}\}$.

\paragraph{6. Longest Repeated Substring (LRS).} With $C_j$ the counts of
$W$-tuples,
\begin{equation}
P_W=\frac{\sum_j\binom{C_j}{2}}{\binom{L-W+1}{2}},\qquad p=\max_{W\ge u}P_W^{1/W}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{7--10. Predictors.} Four predictors try to guess $x_i$ from the
past: MultiMCW (most common value in windows of $63,255,1023,4095$), Lag
(the value $d$ steps back, $d\le128$), MultiMMC (order-$k$ Markov contexts,
$k\le16$) and LZ78Y (longest matching context up to $16$). A scoreboard selects
the subpredictor with the highest accumulated success. From the sequence of
hits (length $N$, $C$ correct):
\begin{equation}
    p_{\rm global}=\hat p+2.576\sqrt{\tfrac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}{N-1}}\ \ (\hat p=C/N),\qquad
    H=-\log_2\max\{p_{\rm global},\,p_{\rm local},\,\tfrac12\},
\end{equation}
where $p_{\rm local}$ is the success probability for which a run of length
$r+1$ ($r$ the longest observed run of hits) appears in $N$ trials with
probability $0.99$. We use Feller's approximation:
$x$ is the root $>1$ of $x=1+q\,p^{r+1}x^{r+2}$ and
$\Pr[\text{no run}]=\frac{1-px}{(r+2-(r+1)x)\,q}\,x^{-(N+1)}$.

\paragraph{Estimate.} $\hat h=\min$ of the ten values, in bits per bit; the total
is $b=L\hat h$.

\paragraph{Deviations from the standard (documented in the code).} Tuples are
capped at $63$ bits; predictors are evaluated on the first $30\,000$ bits (cost
in pure Python); the scoreboard uses cumulative success instead of the
standard's short recent window. The standard requires $10^6$ bits; with $10^5$
the confidence bounds are wider and the estimates are \emph{conservative}
(a perfect source typically yields $0.55$--$0.9$ bits/bit; the variation is
dominated by the local-run bound).

\paragraph{Why the minimum.} The estimators are complementary. In our validation
(Section~\ref{sec:results}) MCV alone gave $0.98$ bits/bit on a source with
memory whose true min-entropy is $0.32$; the collision and predictor estimators
detected the memory. Trusting any single estimator would have overstated the
entropy by a factor of three.

\subsection{Structural tests}

Some structures are invisible to the frequency-based tests.

\paragraph{Binary matrix rank.} Fill $32\times32$ matrices over $\mathbb{F}_2$
with consecutive bits. The ranks are $32$, $31$, $\le30$ with probabilities
$0.2888,0.5776,0.1336$ (for a uniform random matrix), and
$\chi^2=\sum_j(F_j-N_mp_j)^2/(N_mp_j)$, $p=e^{-\chi^2/2}$ ($2$ d.o.f.).
Detects linear dependencies between blocks.

\paragraph{Spectral (DFT).} The magnitudes $|F_k|$, $k<m/2$, of the discrete
Fourier transform of $(X_i)$ should be below $T=\sqrt{\ln(1/0.05)\,m}$ for
$95\%$ of the frequencies. With $N_1$ the observed count below $T$,
\begin{equation}
d=\frac{N_1-0.95\,m/2}{\sqrt{m\cdot0.95\cdot0.05/4}},\qquad p=\erfc(|d|/\sqrt2).
\end{equation}
It detects periodic components.

\paragraph{Approximate entropy.} With $\varphi^{(k)}=\sum_i\pi_i\ln\pi_i$ over
overlapping $k$-bit patterns, $\mathrm{ApEn}(k)=\varphi^{(k)}-\varphi^{(k+1)}$ and
$\chi^2=2m(\ln2-\mathrm{ApEn}(k))\sim\chi^2_{2^k}$ ($k=2$). Detects
non-uniformity of short patterns.

\paragraph{Linear complexity.} The Berlekamp--Massey algorithm~\cite{massey69}
computes the length $L$ of the shortest linear-feedback shift register (LFSR)
over $\mathbb{F}_2$ that generates the sequence: at step $n$ the discrepancy is
$d_n=s_n\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{L}c_is_{n-i}$ and, if $d_n=1$, the connection
polynomial is updated. A random sequence of length $n$ has $L\approx n/2$; an
LFSR-generated sequence has $L$ equal to its register length, so
$L\ll n/2$ once $n>2L$. The deviation $\delta=n/2-L$ has a tail
$\Pr\approx4^{-\delta}$, which we use as a $p$-value approximation.

\paragraph{Strided linear complexity.} A bit-by-bit concatenation of $32$-bit
words hides the structure of generators whose \emph{word} sequence is linear:
their bit stream is a combination of $32$ interleaved sequences, so its
complexity can reach $32\cdot19937\gg n/2$. Extracting \emph{one bit position
of every $32$-bit word} (stride $32$, $n=45\,000$ bits) exposes each
component sequence with $L=19937<n/2=22\,500$. This is the only generic test in
the battery that separates the Mersenne Twister from a CSPRNG.

\subsection{Combining the two axes}\label{sec:quality}

\begin{definition}[Statistical quality]
Let $F$ be the fraction of the seven tests (monobit, runs, block frequency,
forward and backward cumulative sums, longest run, byte chi-squared) that are
OK under both aggregation criteria, and $\hat H_\infty$ the mean per-sample MCV
min-entropy per byte (with the $99\%$ bound). With $H_{\rm ref}$ the same
statistic averaged over $30$ \texttt{os.urandom} samples of the same length
($H_{\rm ref}\approx7.10$ bits/byte at $n=12\,500$, since the confidence bound
is below $8$ at finite $n$),
\begin{equation}
    Q \;=\; 100\Big(0.7\,F+0.3\,\min\{1,\hat H_\infty/H_{\rm ref}\}\Big).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The reference $H_{\rm ref}$ prevents penalizing an ideal source for the finite
sample size.

\begin{definition}[Verdict]
\begin{equation}
    \text{verdict}=
    \begin{cases}
        \text{APTO} & Q\ge85,\ r\ge128\ \text{bits},\\
        \text{IMITA AZAR (mimics randomness)} & Q\ge85,\ r<128,\\
        \text{FALLA TESTS} & Q<85.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The $128$-bit threshold is the security level of secp256k1 (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}).
The threshold $Q\ge85$ tolerates \emph{one} rejected test out of seven ($Q=90$)
and rejects two or more ($Q=80$). It was originally $90$, which classified an
ideal CSPRNG as failing whenever a single test fell below its bound by chance
(this happened in our first complete run: \texttt{secrets} obtained $Q=89.9$).
With $7$ tests and $2$ criteria each, an ideal source rejects at least one
criterion with non-negligible probability, so one rejection is not evidence of
a defect; two independent rejections are.
We deliberately do \emph{not} multiply $Q$ by ad hoc determinism penalties
(the first design used factors $0.5$ and $0.75$): penalties confuse the two
concepts. Statistical quality and real entropy are reported separately, and the
verdict, not a weighted sum, decides suitability.

\subsection{Sources of error in the measurements themselves}

The measurement tools can be wrong, and this materially affects conclusions.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
    \item An integer-overflow bug (\texttt{2*bits-1} in an unsigned 8-bit type)
          made \texttt{os.urandom} fail the monobit test in $100\%$ of the samples
          until we validated each test against the NIST SP~800-22 example
          vectors.
    \item Two statistics with the same identifier overwrote each other in the
          aggregation dictionary (forward and backward cumulative sums).
    \item With $n=256$ bytes the chi-squared test was invalid ($E_v=1<5$), and
          mean MCV min-entropy was capped near $5$ bits/byte by sample size.
    \item Reference vectors also validate the cryptographic pipeline (BIP32,
          BIP39, bech32, descriptor checksums, xpub serialization).
\end{itemize}

\section{Attacks on Generators that Pass the Battery}\label{sec:attacks}
% ============================================================

The following attacks are implemented (\texttt{entropy\_arena/attacks}) and
tested. Each uses only information available to the adversary of
Definition~\ref{def:adv}.

\paragraph{State recovery of MT19937.} Each output word $y=T(x)$ with $T$ the
invertible tempering. From $624$ consecutive words ($19\,968\ge19\,937$ state
bits), $x=T^{-1}(y)$ is computed by undoing the shift-and-mask steps
(each recovered in $\le32$ iterations of $x\leftarrow y\oplus(x\ll s\wedge m)$).
Loading the recovered state into a clone predicts all future outputs. It
recovers the state from $2\,496$ bytes in $0.02$~s; the same attack on
\texttt{secrets} fails, as expected.

\paragraph{State recovery of RANDU.} Observing the $8$ high bits of each
$31$-bit state, the unknown $23$ low bits of the first state are enumerated
($2^{23}$ candidates); each subsequent observation keeps a candidate with
probability $2^{-8}$, so $\approx4$ observations leave one. It succeeds from
$8$ bytes in $\approx0.3$~s.

\paragraph{Offset of the digits of $\pi$.} The ``seed'' is an offset in a public
constant ($\log_2\approx22.9$ bits); a string search of $32$ observed bytes
locates it.

\paragraph{Clock-seeded generators.} If the generator is seeded with
\texttt{int(time.time())}, the search space for a day is $86\,400$ seeds; brute
force finds the seed in at most $\approx0.6$~s.

\paragraph{Linear complexity} (Section~\ref{sec:metrics}) is the statistical
counterpart of the MT attack: $2L=39\,874$ bits of a single bit position
determine the recurrence.

These results give the operational meaning of the $r$ axis: $r<128$ is not an
abstract number, it is the distance to an attack that runs in seconds.

\section{Cryptographic Pipeline}\label{sec:pipeline}
% ============================================================

\subsection{From entropy to seed (BIP39)}

$256$ bits of entropy $E$ are encoded as a $24$-word mnemonic $\mathsf{m}$
(the entropy plus an $8$-bit SHA-256 checksum, in groups of $11$ bits indexing
a $2048$-word list). The seed is derived from the \emph{mnemonic sentence},
not from the raw entropy:
\begin{equation}
    S=\mathrm{PBKDF2\text{-}HMAC\text{-}SHA512}\big(\mathsf{m},\,\texttt{"mnemonic"}\|P,\,2048,\,64\big).
\end{equation}
(The first prototype derived it from the raw bytes, a deviation from the
standard that would make wallets incompatible with any other software.)
The generator \emph{refuses} to build a signer if the source reports
$r<128$ bits.

\subsection{Hierarchical derivation (BIP32)}

With $I=\mathrm{HMAC\text{-}SHA512}(\texttt{"Bitcoin seed"},S)$, the master key
is $(k_m,c_m)=(I_L,I_R)$. The child $i$ of $(k,c)$ is
\begin{equation}
    I=\mathrm{HMAC}\big(c,\;\underbrace{0\mathrm{x}00\|k}_{i\ge2^{31}}\ \text{or}\ \underbrace{\mathrm{ser}_P(kG)}_{i<2^{31}}\ \|\ i\big),\qquad
    k_i=(I_L+k)\bmod n_G,\quad c_i=I_R,
\end{equation}
where $G$ is the secp256k1 base point and $n_G$ its order. The \emph{public}
derivation (CKDpub) computes $K_i=I_L G+K$ from the parent public key only and
is defined solely for non-hardened indices. This is the mathematical basis of
watch-only wallets: from an extended public key one derives all receiving
addresses but no private key; hardened steps stop an extended public key from
revealing sibling private keys.

The account path is BIP48, $m/48'/c'/0'/2'$ ($c=0$ mainnet, $c=1$
testnet/regtest; script type $2$ = P2WSH). Extended keys are serialized
(Base58Check) as
\begin{equation}
\text{version}\,\|\,\text{depth}\,\|\,\text{parent fp.}\,\|\,\text{index}\,\|\,\text{chain code}\,\|\,\text{key},
\end{equation}
where the \emph{fingerprint} is the first four bytes of
$\mathrm{RIPEMD160}(\mathrm{SHA256}(K))$ of the master public key.

\subsection{Multisignature script and descriptors}

For threshold $t$ and public keys $K_1,\dots,K_n$ sorted lexicographically
(BIP67), the witness script is
\begin{equation}
    \texttt{OP\_}t\ \langle K_{(1)}\rangle\cdots\langle K_{(n)}\rangle\ \texttt{OP\_}n\ \texttt{OP\_CHECKMULTISIG},
\end{equation}
and the output is the P2WSH program $\mathrm{SHA256}(\texttt{script})$,
encoded in bech32 (BIP173, a BCH code with polymod checksum). The script
accepts a transaction $T$ iff there exists $\mathcal{S}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}$,
$|\mathcal{S}|\ge t$, with $\mathrm{Verify}(K_i,T,\sigma_i)=1$ for $i\in\mathcal{S}$.
The public wallet configuration is the \emph{output descriptor}
\[
\texttt{wsh(sortedmulti(2,[fp}_1\texttt{/48h/1h/0h/2h]tpub}_1\texttt{/<0;1>/*,\ldots))\#}c,
\]
whose $8$-character checksum $c$ is a BCH code (BIP380) that detects transcription errors.
It contains fingerprints, origin paths, extended public keys and policy, and
none of the private material.

\subsection{Verification chain}

Every layer is validated against independent implementations: BIP32 test
vector~1 (xpub, xprv, fingerprint), BIP39 (\texttt{TREZOR} vector), BIP173, the
BIP380 checksum vector; and end-to-end against Bitcoin Core in \texttt{regtest}:
three signers with separate wallets (each holding only its own \texttt{tprv}),
a watch-only coordinator whose address coincides with our public derivation,
a funded PSBT signed independently by two signers, a rejected finalization with
one signature, and the confirmed transaction whose witness is
$[\varnothing,\sigma_a,\sigma_b,\text{witnessScript}]$.

\section{Security Composition in Threshold Schemes}
% ============================================================

Let the $n$ signers use independent keys with min-entropies
$h_1\le h_2\le\dots\le h_n$ (bits). A key with $h$ bits of entropy costs the
adversary $\approx2^{h}$ trials; but a public key also carries an ECDLP
barrier of $\approx2^{128}$ operations, so the effective strength of one key is
$\min\{h_i,128\}$.

\begin{proposition}[$t$-of-$n$ security]\label{prop:tofn}
To spend, the adversary must recover $t$ keys. Attacking the $t$ weakest keys,
the expected work is $\sum_{i\le t}2^{\min(h_i,128)}\approx2^{\min(h_{(t)},128)}$,
where $h_{(t)}=h_t$ is the $t$-th smallest min-entropy. The wallet's security
(in bits) is therefore $\min\{h_{(t)},128\}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Correction of a common belief]
The security of a $t$-of-$n$ scheme is \emph{not} that of the strongest
source. For $2$-of-$3$, a single strong key is not enough: if two sources are
weak, the adversary breaks both and spends without the strong key. To have
$s$-bit security we need at least $n-t+1$ strong keys ($=2$ for $2$-of-$3$).
Equivalently: the design tolerates the compromise of up to $t-1$ keys
\emph{after} generation (theft, loss), but requires that at least $n-t+1$ keys
be generated with strong entropy.
\end{corollary}

The earlier version of this document claimed
$P_{\rm guess}\le2^{-H^\ast_\infty}$ if a single source is honest; that is true
for an $n$-of-$n$ (unanimity) scheme, where the adversary needs \emph{every}
key, not for $t$-of-$n$. The code implements
Proposition~\ref{prop:tofn} as the ``attack effort'' reported by the vault
builder.

\paragraph{Loss and compromise model.} With a $2$-of-$3$ policy: losing one key
leaves the funds spendable by the other two (a migration is advisable); losing
two locks the funds; compromising one key does not allow spending; compromising
two does. Independence of the signers (separate machines, separate entropy
sources) is a requirement of the argument, not just of the procedure.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{Why imitating randomness does not make a model good.}
Statistical tests measure a property of the \emph{distribution of outputs}
($D$ looks like $U_n$ to the tested statistics); cryptographic security is the
\emph{adversary's guessing probability given what he knows}, i.e.\ conditional
min-entropy. For a deterministic $g$ with a seed of $s$ bits,
Theorem~\ref{thm:conservation} bounds the latter by $s$ whatever the former.
The two are decoupled in both directions (UUIDv4 fails tests with high entropy;
the Mersenne Twister passes tests with none), so a single score conflates two
questions.

\paragraph{Why the physical sources work.} A physical source injects entropy
that is not a function of a bounded seed: thermal noise, timing jitter,
chaotic mechanical amplification. Its difficulties are not statistical but
\emph{metrological}: bias (coin $0.508$), serial correlation (jitter, dice
habits), and, above all, the difficulty of \emph{certifying} the entropy rate.
Hence the two-step design: measure a conservative min-entropy of the raw
outcomes, then condition with a hash for uniform bytes, never taking the hash
output's statistics as evidence of entropy.

\paragraph{Why simulated physics does not work.} Brownian motion and the Lorenz
flow are physical models, but a computer executes a deterministic
approximation with a bounded seed. Entropy enters only through the seed; the
model can amplify it (Lorenz, at $h_{KS}$ bits per unit time) but not create
it, and oversampling beyond $h_{KS}$ reads the same information repeatedly.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
    \item The estimators are adapted, not certified, and use $10^5$ bits
          (the standard asks $10^6$); estimates are conservative.
    \item The battery is a subset of NIST SP~800-22 plus four structural tests;
          it does not include Diehard/TestU01/PractRand.
    \item A manual session is one fixed sample: uniformity of $p$-values does not
          apply and the NIST proportions are unreliable (the arena warns).
    \item Humans do not shuffle or flip uniformly; the deck bits are an upper
          bound, and dice/coin bits are measured lower bounds at $99\%$ confidence.
    \item Elliptic-curve arithmetic is pure Python and not side-channel
          resistant: the pipeline is intended for regtest/testnet only.
    \item An ideal source may fail one test out of seven by chance ($\sim1$--$2\%$
          per criterion).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Conclusions.} (i) $Q$ and $r$ must be reported separately.
(ii) The only sources that supply key entropy are those with a fresh
unpredictable input of at least $128$ bits: a CSPRNG (OS entropy pool),
or a physically measured source whose min-entropy has been certified;
mathematical maps, Mersenne Twister, RANDU, $\pi$ and cellular automata are
deterministic and only propagate their seed. (iii) A $t$-of-$n$ wallet is as
strong as the $t$-th weakest key. (iv) Every measurement tool must be
validated against known vectors: several of the phenomena reported by the
arena were, at first, defects of the arena.
